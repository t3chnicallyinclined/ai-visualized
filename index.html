1Your words become pieces

The picture: a big dictionary of pieces, each with a number. The model never sees letters, only these numbers.

2Each piece gets its sound

The picture: each piece goes in as its own “sound”: a list of numbers. Not how the word is pronounced; it comes from how the word is used, so words used alike sound alike to the model.

3One layer, up close

The knobs

Its sound, before and after this layer

The picture: rows of volume knobs, set at the factory and glued. Before each row, the newest word listens back to the earlier words (pink) and blends in what matters. That listening is the only step whose mixing depends on what you type.

4Through every layer

The thought cloud: every piece in the dictionary is a dot, and pieces used alike sit close together.

The picture: the same thing, row after row of knobs. Next to each layer: what it would say if it stopped there (the “logit lens”), and in the cloud, every piece it might say lights up. Early layers are not built to be read this way, so their guesses look odd. The cloud is a flat map of the model's own embedding rows (made with t-SNE); distances on it are approximate.

5The wall of lights

The same wall of lights, laid out as the thought cloud.

The picture: a wall with one light for every piece in the dictionary. Each glows by how well the final sound matches that piece's row in the output table.

6The weighted dice

🎲

The picture: a dice loaded by the brightest lights. Usually the brightest wins; sometimes a dimmer one does. That is why the same start can give different stories.
